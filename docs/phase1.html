Microtomy and staining: a histological study of tissues

A hands-on workshop in the Department of Zoology: embedding rat tissue in wax, cutting 7–8 µm sections on a manual rotary microtome, staining them with haematoxylin and eosin, and reading the result under a light microscope. The work was presented as a group poster. I took part as one of fifteen student authors, and the protocol below is the one on that poster.

Phase 2, the computational follow-up, is here.

The protocol, step by step

Written out as a procedure, with the numbers that were actually used. Each step exists to stop a specific thing going wrong, which is the second column.

StepWhat was doneWhy
Tissue extractionTissue placed in 0.9% salineIsotonic with body fluid, so cells neither swell nor shrink
FixationBouin's fixative (25 ml formalin, 70 ml picric acid, 5 ml glacial acetic acid)Cross-links protein and stops autolysis, which would otherwise destroy the architecture being studied
WashingOvernight running wash, then distilled waterRemoves fixative; leftover picric acid interferes with staining
DehydrationAscending alcohol, 1 h each: 30% → 50% → 70% → 90% → 100% (1½ h), then xylene until clearWax will not enter wet tissue; the gradient avoids the shrinkage a sudden change causes
Wax infiltrationXylene–paraffin 1:1, then three changes of seasoned paraffin, 1 h eachReplaces the clearing agent with wax throughout, so the block cuts evenly
EmbeddingFresh cavity block, glycerine-coated, left to solidify; trimmed and mounted on woodGives a block that can be clamped and oriented
SectioningManual rotary microtome, 7–8 µm; ribbons floated at 57–58 °C and picked up on albumin-coated slidesThin enough for light to pass through a single cell layer; the warm bath flattens the compression from cutting
StainingXylene ×2 (15 min each) → descending alcohol 100 → 90 → 70 → 50 → 30% (5 min each) → haematoxylin 35 s → alcohol → eosin 2–3 dips → DPX and coverslipDewax, rehydrate, stain nuclei blue and cytoplasm pink, then dehydrate and seal. The times are the part that was standardised.

What we saw

  • Permanent H&E slides of rat tissue; the poster shows a transverse section of duodenum.
  • All four wall layers were resolved: serosa, muscularis, submucosa and mucosa, with villi and crypts, and Brunner's glands in the submucosa.
  • Nuclei blue from the haematoxylin, cytoplasm pink from the eosin — the contrast the whole method exists to produce.
  • Staining was standardised at 35 seconds of haematoxylin and one dip of eosin, after comparing shorter and longer times by eye.

Why this led to phase 2

The one decision we made by eye was how long to stain. That is fine when a person is going to look at the slide, because the eye adapts to the overall colour. It is not obviously fine when software is going to measure the slide instead, and that is a question the workshop could not answer with the slides in front of us: we had no way of knowing the true number of nuclei in a field, so we could not tell whether a measurement had changed because the tissue differed or because the staining did.

Phase 2 answers it by building fields whose nuclei are known exactly, staining them computationally at strengths from 0.4× to 2.0×, and measuring what happens. The result is here: the staining matters enormously to a fixed cut-off and hardly at all to a method that re-derives its threshold per image — so the protocol was right to pin the times down, and software reading such slides should not assume they were.
